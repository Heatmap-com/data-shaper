## 1. Desktop stopped buying in the week of 5 Oct

The homepage's three full weeks sit in a band: order rate 1.5% to 2.2%, revenue per session $10 to $13, about half of sessions leaving after one page, about 40% reaching a quarter of the page. The week of 5 Oct leaves that band. Revenue per session falls from **$13.30** to **$7.44**, and the order rate from **2.15%** to **1.08%**. The short week explains a smaller session count. It does not explain the orders.

Desktop is where the money leaves, and the sessions stay.

| Homepage entry | Week of 28 Sep | Week of 5 Oct |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Desktop, all sources | 1,235 sessions, $19.74 each | 1,123 sessions, $5.62 each |
| Desktop direct | 642 sessions, 23 orders, $16,796 | 603 sessions, 8 orders, $3,280 |
| Desktop campaign | 148 sessions, 5 orders, $5,596 | 141 sessions, 4 orders, $1,852 |
| Desktop search | 373 sessions, 5 orders, $1,986 | 313 sessions, 1 order, $463 |
| Mobile direct | 899 sessions, 43 orders, $24,506 | 658 sessions, 17 orders, $20,058 |

Desktop direct kept its traffic (642 sessions, then 603) and lost the orders (23, then 8). Campaign and search on desktop moved the same way, on smaller bases. Mobile direct revenue held at $20,058 on the shorter week. The blended mobile-to-desktop ratio of 0.90 hides this split. In the week of 28 Sep, desktop earned $19.74 per session against mobile's $12.83. In the week of 5 Oct, desktop earned $5.62 against mobile's $9.93.

The same week shows up on the path `plp > pdp`, which is a different population from homepage entries. Desktop on that path went from **163 sessions, 4 buyers, $1,506** to **327 sessions, 0 buyers, $0**. Sessions rose. Purchases went to zero. The whole path went from 1,363 sessions and $13,077 to 987 sessions and $3,996. The session drop matches a five-day week. The buyer drop, 28 to 7, does not.

Meta on that path is a mobile fact sitting beside this, and it is a different slice. Meta was 334 sessions and $381 in the week of 28 Sep, 313 of them mobile, one buyer. The week of 5 Oct is 89 sessions and $0, 79 of them mobile. Search on the same path held its sessions (249, then 295) and its buyers fell from 7 to 2.

**Opportunity.** Treat the week of 5 Oct as a desktop purchase gap, with homepage direct as the sharpest cut: traffic in place, orders down from 23 to 8. Put the path's desktop zero next to it as a second reading of the same week, and keep Meta's mobile path as its own question. A layout change on the homepage does not follow from this until those two cuts are explained.

## 3. Abandoned-cart visitors are a recovery cohort

A cart hit is a session with an add-to-cart or a checkout start, and no purchase. The chain follows that visitor for 14 days. Closed hits run from 10 Sep through 25 Sep. Later days are still open.

Counted once per visitor, with a blank visitor id left out:

| | Visitors | Bought later | Came back |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| All closed cart hits | 2,699 | 221 (8.2%) | 1,370 (51%) |
| Mobile hits | 1,814 | 136 (7.5%) | 894 |
| Desktop hits | 451 | 45 (10.0%) | 255 |
| Tablet hits | 474 | 51 (10.8%) | 256 |

A visitor who hits on two devices is in both device rows. About eleven buyers appear in more than one device, so the device buyer counts add up a little past 221.

The chain rows say 364 of 3,356 hits purchased (11%) and carry **$235,354**. That adds one visitor once per cart session. Kept once per visitor, at that visitor's largest chain, the same buyers carry **$142,144**.

Showed-interest visitors are the wide net: 17,115 closed visitors, and 282 of them bought on a later visit (1.6%), close to the homepage's own order rate. The cart cohort is where later purchase concentrates.

**Opportunity.** Size a recovery message on the 221 buyers and the $142,144, not on the 11% chain rate or the $235,354. Mobile is two-thirds of the visitors and recovers less often than desktop or tablet. The open chains from 26 Sep onward are early and will move on the next run.

## What the triggered rules do after they are named

### The hero is on screen, and about 1% of sessions click it

The CTA's top is above the fold on **99.8%** of entry sessions. Across the month the hero has **269** clicking sessions and **3** orders, all three in the week of 28 Sep. At the homepage's order rate for the month, about 1.7%, those 269 sessions would have produced about four or five orders. The stored rule compared the month's hero rate (1.12%) with a shorter page window and called the clickers weak. The count is three orders against about five. That is too close to treat the clickers as a conversion problem.

The click itself is the stable fact. Roughly **1%** of homepage entry sessions click the hero, a bit higher on desktop than on mobile, and that rate does not break in the week of 5 Oct.

**Opportunity.** Leave the fold alone. A hero test, if one is run, is about why a control that is on screen draws about 1% of sessions, not about the three orders those sessions produced.
