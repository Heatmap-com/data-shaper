## The biggest wins, first

Five moves cover most of what this month can support. Two are about traffic the site is already paying to receive. One recovers shoppers who already put something in a bag. One uses a page that already sells. One keeps a set of landing URLs from distorting the rest.

1. **Judge Meta on the comforter URL against Google on the same URL.** Meta campaign links sent 737,715 visits to `/products/marshmellow-comforter`. Those visits earned $1.35 each and ordered 0.9% of the time. Google paid, on that same URL, earned $3.22 and ordered 2.1% of the time. Untagged arrivals on the same URL earned $5.01 and ordered 3.2% of the time. The gap between Meta and Google on this one address is about $1.4 million in the month. That is the size of the difference, not a claim that every Meta visit can be turned into a Google visit. (Opportunity 2)

2. **Turn off, or move, the landers that do not sell.** Five URLs took 410,387 landings and produced 601 orders from those landings, about $0.23 of order value per landing. Three of them, the `-amz` addresses, produced 21 orders from 279,155 landings. (Opportunity 5)

3. **Build the bag-recovery flow for the full two weeks, on the phone.** Among abandoned-cart visits whose 14 days had already closed, 10.6% bought. Those purchases total $2,880,617, about $137 each. Just under half of the visits came back at all. One further point of recovery, on this same base, is about 1,970 purchases and about $270,000. Nine in ten of the visits are on a phone. (Opportunity 1)

4. **Hand the comforter landing to the shop page, a second product, or a bundle.** A visit that sees one product page orders 2.9% of the time and is worth $3.46. A visit that sees two product pages orders 10.0% of the time and is worth $13.21. The shop page is already that second step: a landing there orders 3.6% of the time, against 1.0% for a comforter landing, on the same counting method. (Opportunity 3)

5. **Point the $190 buyer at the pages they already use.** Orders of $190 or more are 11,887 of the month, 9.9% of orders and $2,899,206, a fifth of revenue. High-value buyers reach `/collections/bundles` at 16 times the rate of all visitors, and the cooling sheet set at 21 times. The comforter is where most of them also go, because almost everyone goes there. The bundles and the sheets are where they concentrate. (Opportunity 4)

## Opportunity 1 — Bring back the shoppers who leave a bag

### What we saw

Every visit that left a bag was followed for 14 days. The clean read is the visits whose 14 days had already closed by the time of this analysis: 197,188 visits.

| Device | Visits | Bought within 14 days | Rate | Came back |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| All | 197,188 | 20,968 | 10.6% | 47% |
| Mobile | 177,968 (90%) | 18,816 | 10.6% | 47% |
| Tablet | 10,627 | 1,224 | 11.5% | 45% |
| Desktop | 8,593 | 928 | 10.8% | 42% |

- The 20,968 purchases total **$2,880,617**, about **$137** each. That is above the site average of $121.
- Mobile recovers at the same rate as desktop. The phone matters because it holds 90% of the visits, not because it recovers worse.
- Just under half the visits come back. About a third come back and still leave without buying. The flow has a second chance with people who already returned, and it is not converting them.
- A larger set of 394,617 abandoned-cart visits, including visits whose 14 days were still open, shows 9.8% buying and $5.25 million attached. The open visits have not had their full two weeks, so the 10.6% figure is the one to manage against.

### Why it matters

One more point of recovery on the closed set is about 1,970 purchases. At the $137 those purchases already show, that point is about **$270,000**. The current 10.6% is the base, not the ceiling: a third of the audience comes back and does not buy.

These are visits, not unique people. A shopper who leaves a bag twice is counted twice, and one later order can be attached to more than one visit. The rate is still the right weekly number. The dollar figure should be read as the value attached to those visits, not as 20,968 guaranteed distinct buyers.

### Recommended actions

1. **Run the recovery across the full 14 days**, not as a single same-day email. The purchases that define the 10.6% happened somewhere in that window, and nearly half the audience does come back.
2. **Design it for a phone.** Nine in ten of these visits are mobile. SMS or push where consent exists, with a link that opens the saved bag.
3. **Treat the return visit as its own step.** A third come back and do not buy. The return should show the bag, not the homepage.
4. **Track one number:** the share of abandoned-cart visits that buy within 14 days, using only visits whose window has closed. Today that is 10.6%.

## Opportunity 2 — Meta is buying the comforter page at a third of the price the page already earns

### What we saw

`/products/marshmellow-comforter` is the busiest product URL on the site. Among human visits that landed on it, the campaign link decides the value.

| Arrival on the comforter URL | Visits | Order rate | Revenue per visit | Revenue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Meta campaign link | 737,715 | 0.9% | $1.35 | $993,247 |
| Google paid | 76,892 | 2.1% | $3.22 | $247,278 |
| No campaign link | 188,027 | 3.2% | $5.01 | $941,518 |
| Meta, no campaign tag | 56,512 | 3.7% | $5.92 | $334,648 |
| Email or SMS | 15,506 | 4.0% | $5.94 | $92,138 |

Across the whole site, the same pattern holds. Meta campaign links are 1,732,128 human visits, a 1.4% order rate, and $1.66 per visit ($2,876,599). Search, as a referrer, is 511,096 visits, a 5.2% order rate, and $6.70 per visit ($3,424,978). Search produces more revenue than Meta on less than a third of the visits.

Meta's rate did not collapse during the month. It sat between 1.3% and 1.5% in every week from 24 August through 10 October, and revenue per visit moved only between $1.35 and $1.78. This is the normal result of the current Meta program, not a bad week.

Google paid on the site is steadier and richer: about $3.05 to $3.82 per visit through the window, 133,215 visits, $429,108.

### Why it matters

On one URL, the gap between Meta at $1.35 and Google at $3.22 is **$1.87 a visit**. Across 737,715 visits that is about **$1.4 million** in the month. Untagged arrivals on the same URL, at $5.01, show that the page can sell when the visit is not a cold paid click. The page is not the whole problem. The traffic being bought into it is.

None of that $1.4 million should be written into a forecast as money to be collected next month. Prospecting traffic will not match a branded search visit. The useful claim is narrower: the same product URL already earns more than twice as much from another paid channel, so the Meta campaigns that land there should be ranked against that bar, campaign by campaign, and the losers should stop.

### Recommended actions

1. **List every Meta campaign and ad set whose landing URL is the comforter**, with visits, order rate, and revenue per visit. Pause what falls well below the Google-on-this-URL bar of about $3.20.
2. **Test the shop page as the Meta landing** on a slice of the budget. A shop landing converts at 3.6% against 1.0% for a comforter landing, on a count that includes one-page visits. The shop is where a cold visit can still see a second product.
3. **Keep Google paid on its current path** while that test runs. It is the paid channel that already earns a mid-three-dollar visit.
4. **Do not read sitewide conversion as the Meta number.** Meta is more than half of tagged paid visits. A blended rate hides a 1.4% channel inside a 3.0% site.

## Opportunity 3 — The shop page sells; the comforter landing does not hand the shopper on

### What we saw

Landing-page order rates below include one-page visits, so they sit lower than the human-visit rates elsewhere. Every page is counted the same way, and a visit has one landing page.

| Landing URL | Landings | Orders from that landing | Rate | Visits that reached the page and bought |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `/pages/shop` | 400,888 | 14,556 | 3.6% | 6.6% of 712,368 visits |
| `/products/cloud-alignment-pillow` | 454,012 | 8,090 | 1.8% | 4.3% of 599,754 visits |
| `/products/marshmellow-comforter` | 1,006,280 | 10,190 | 1.0% | 2.2% of 1,181,378 visits |
| `/collections/bundles` | 11,140 | 491 | 4.4% | 11.1% of 69,458 visits |
| Home | — | — | — | 8.1% order rate, $10.33 per human visit, on 356,070 entry visits |

Among human visits in the window:

| Product pages seen | Visits | Order rate | Revenue per visit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| None | 975,487 | 1.0% | $1.13 |
| One | 1,777,055 | 2.9% | $3.46 |
| Two | 174,405 | 10.0% | $13.21 |
| Three or four | 60,492 | 14.5% | $22.70 |

The home entry is the most valuable common door on a per-visit basis. The comforter is the most common door, and one of the least valuable. The pillow page, with half the landings, converts nearly twice as well. The bundle collection is small and strong.

### Why it matters

Most of the paid program lands on a single product and stops. The visits that reach a second product page are worth about four times as much. The shop page is the existing version of that second step, at 3.6% of landings against 1.0% for the comforter. Moving even a modest share of comforter landings onto a path that shows a second product is the merchandising version of Opportunity 2.

The overlap caveat matters for revenue attached to a page. A visit that orders is counted on every page it touched, so the shop page's $6.15 million and the comforter's $4.11 million are not two piles of money. Landing-page order counts do not overlap. Those are the figures to act on.

### Recommended actions

1. **On the comforter, above the fold on mobile, offer one next product:** the CloudAlign pillow, a bundle, or a jump to the shop. The phone is 89% of visits. The module has to be in the thumb zone.
2. **Use the shop page as a landing destination** for a defined share of Meta, as in Opportunity 2, and keep it fast. It already does the job the comforter landing does not.
3. **Give the pillow page the same care as the comforter.** It converts better on a landing, and orders that record it as the main line grew in the second half of the month while comforter orders did not.
4. **Read success as the share of comforter landings that view a second product**, and as landing order rate. Not as clicks on the module.

## Opportunity 4 — The $190 order is a different visit, and the site already has the pages for it

### What we saw

The average order is $121 and the median is $113. The order file bunches under that average.

| --- | --- | --- |
| $50–80 | 29,608 | $2,029,125 |
| $80–100 | 14,044 | $1,243,042 |
| $100–120 | 24,928 | $2,792,594 |

| $150–190 | 14,616 | $2,513,351 |
| $190 and up | 11,887 | $2,899,206 |

Orders of $190 or more are 9.9% of orders and 19.9% of revenue.

High-value buyers, by device id, do not spread evenly. Compared with all visitors they are:

- 21 times as likely to reach the cooling sheet set
- 16 times as likely to reach `/collections/bundles`
- 5.7 times as likely to reach the home page
- 4.7 times as likely to reach the shop page
- 2.6 times as likely to reach the comforter, which is high reach rather than high concentration: 70% of them get there because 26% of everyone does

The recorded line item is one line per order, the highest-priced line stored, so this is not a full basket. Even so, the line prices tell a simple story. Comforter orders record a modal price of $114.99 (6,086 orders) and a second price of $104.99 (5,923 orders). Both prices ran in both halves of the month. Pillow orders record $54.99. Orders whose recorded line is the pillow went from 14,508 in the first half to 17,816 in the second. Comforter line-orders stayed at 11,717 and then 11,721.

### Why it matters

There is no $250 cliff to build a promotion around. The value sits in a fifth of revenue coming from a tenth of orders, on pages the site already has. Bundles and sheets are the attach. The comforter's two live prices, $10 apart and equal in volume, are a standing discount rather than an event. Cutting the $114.99 price further is not what the month argues for. Showing the sheet set and the bundle to the shopper who is already on the comforter is.

### Recommended actions

1. **Put a bundle and the cooling sheet set on the comforter page and the shop page**, in the same view as the main product on mobile.
2. **Watch the share of orders at $190 or more**, and the landing rate on `/collections/bundles`. That is the score for this work.
3. **Leave the $104.99 and $114.99 pair in place until there is a test.** Both ran all month at similar volume. The data does not say the lower price is new, or that it should become the only price.
4. **Do not brief this as a threshold-bar program.** The mass of orders is already between $50 and $150. A bar set far above the median will not catch the 29,608 orders sitting at $50–80.

## Opportunity 5 — Keep the dead landings and the one-page visits out of the decision

### What we saw

Five URLs took 410,387 landings and 601 orders from those landings. Buyer-visit revenue on them is $93,441, about $0.23 per landing.

| Landing URL | Landings | Orders from that landing | Rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `/pages/marshmellow-comforter-amz` | 117,028 | 15 | 0.01% |
| `/pages/solvi-list-1` | 93,331 | 431 | 0.46% |
| `/pages/gu-ad-1-amz` | 91,986 | 2 | 0.00% |
| `/pages/lp-hpil-perf-hotel-amz` | 70,141 | 4 | 0.01% |
| `/pages/lp-marsh-cool-cozy-1` | 37,901 | 149 | 0.39% |

The three `-amz` URLs alone are 279,155 landings and 21 orders.

Separately, 1,656,280 visits in the window were a single page with no click, no scroll, and no order. That is 35.6% of raw visits (4,658,607). They are excluded from every human-visit rate in this report. They are still inside the landing-page table above, which is built from page views. A landing count and a human-visit count are not the same number.

Two advertorials sit just above the worst of this and still convert poorly: `/pages/gu-ad-1` at 1.0% of 223,711 landings, and `/pages/heim-adv-1` at 0.6% of 184,277 landings.

This extract does not identify a single automated fingerprint the way a bot report would. The one-page visits are a mix of bounces and automation. They should not be briefed as one botnet.

### Why it matters

Anyone reading a raw conversion rate on those `-amz` URLs, or on a week when the one-page share moves, will understate the pages that actually sell. There is also a spend question this file cannot close: if those URLs are paid destinations, they are being bought at roughly a cent of on-site order value per visit. If they are meant to hand the shopper to Amazon, the on-site order file is the wrong scorecard, and someone should say so before the next budget meeting.

### Recommended actions

1. **Name the owner of the three `-amz` URLs.** If they are paid, pause them or point them at the shop page. If they are an Amazon handoff, take them out of on-site conversion reporting.
2. **Rank `/pages/gu-ad-1` and `/pages/heim-adv-1` with the Meta audit in Opportunity 2.** A 0.6–1.0% landing rate is the same problem as the comforter's Meta traffic, on a weaker page.
3. **Keep one-page, no-engagement visits out of the weekly conversion line**, and show their share next to it. When that share moves, every other rate needs a second look.
4. **Do not block traffic on this evidence alone.** The file shows visits that do not buy. It does not show a fingerprint to ban.

## Worth watching

**The step down from the prior 30 days.** 12 August – 10 September ran 5,202 orders a day, a $112 average, and $583,000 a day. This window ran 4,009, $121, and $485,000. One month-to-month drop is not a trend. If the next 30 days stay near 4,000 orders, the average-order gain is not paying for the lost volume, and Opportunities 1 and 3 are the commercial response.

**The pillow is growing, the comforter is not.** Orders whose recorded line is the CloudAlign pillow rose from 14,508 to 17,816 between the two halves. Comforter line-orders were 11,717 and then 11,721. The pillow page also converts better as a landing (1.8% against 1.0%). The recorded line is the highest-priced line stored, not the whole basket, so this is a shift in what the order file names, and it is large enough to merchandise for.

**Two comforter prices, all month.** $114.99 on 6,086 orders and $104.99 on 5,923, with $124.99 on a further 3,754. The lower price is not a second-half event. Any test should be explicit. The current pair is an accident of the catalogue until someone owns it.

**Email revenue per visit cooled and then firmed.** Campaign email and SMS earned $6.53 per visit in the week of 14 September, $5.78 and $5.79 in the weeks of 21 and 28 September, and $7.91 in the week of 5 October. It remains one of the best scaled channels. The mid-month dip is the thing to explain, not a reason to cut the program.

**Saturday carries the week.** 25,301 of 120,280 orders fell on a Saturday. 10 October, at 7,475 orders, is the top of that pattern. Creative, sends, and landing-page changes should be judged on a Saturday, because that is when the volume is there.

## What's working

These held up under the same cuts as the opportunities. None of them needs a project this month.

- **The rate is steady inside the window.** Full weeks sat between 26,000 and 29,000 orders, and the average order stayed between $119 and $124. The business is readable. The change that matters is against the prior 30 days, not inside this one.
- **The shop page.** A 3.6% landing order rate, against 1.0% on the comforter, on the same counting method. It is the page Opportunity 3 is trying to imitate.
- **Search and untagged visits.** Search referrers earn $6.70 a visit. Visits with no campaign link earn $7.43. They are the benchmark paid has to approach, not a problem to fix.
- **Email and SMS.** Even in the soft weeks they earned about $5.80 a visit, and they ordered on 4.7% to 6.8% of visits. The audience is small next to Meta and much more productive.
- **Google paid.** About $3.20 a visit, week after week, including on the comforter URL itself.
- **Mobile checkout.** Completion is 41.8% on mobile against 39.0% on desktop, and add-to-bag rates match. There is no mobile-checkout rescue hiding in this month.
- **Click rage is not the story.** On the comforter page, frustrated clicks are a rounding error next to ordinary clicks. The commercial gap is the landing and the second product, not a broken control.
- **Recovery already works at 10.6%.** That is a base to extend across 14 days, not a program to replace.

## Method and caveats

- Orders are the recorded total on mellowsleep.com from 11 September through 10 October 2026. The first week in the table starts on the 11th. The last week ends on the 10th. The prior-30-day comparison is 12 August through 10 September, not the same dates last year.
- Human-visit rates exclude visits with one page or none, no click, no scroll, and no order. There were 1,656,280 of those in the window, 35.6% of raw visits. Landing-page tables built from page views still include them. Where a landing rate is cited, the report says so.
- Of 120,280 orders, 91,491 matched a human visit. Order totals and average order use every order. Revenue per visit uses orders that matched a visit, so it will not add up to $14.6 million.
- A campaign link is the tagged source, medium, and campaign on the visit. A referrer channel is where the browser said it came from. Meta appears in both, and the counts differ (1.73 million campaign-link visits against 1.64 million paid-referrer visits). Each table uses one of the two and names it.
- Abandoned-cart figures are visits, not people. A shopper who leaves a bag more than once is counted more than once, and one later purchase can be attached to more than one visit. The 10.6% uses only visits whose 14-day window had closed.
- The order file stores one line per order, the highest-priced line it holds. Product shares, the pillow-versus-comforter shift, and the $104.99 / $114.99 split are shares of that line, not of a full basket. Attach recommendations use page reach and the count of product pages seen, not a line-item join.
- High-value buyers are device ids with an order of $190 or more, the 90th percentile of order value in the window rounded to the nearest $10. A person on two devices counts twice.
- Buyer-visit revenue on a page counts the order if the visit reached the page. It is not revenue attributed to the page. Landing-page orders do not have that overlap, because a visit has one landing URL.
- No ad-cost file was in this analysis. Revenue per visit is not return on ad spend. A campaign can look poor here and still clear its cost target, or the reverse. The $1.4 million Meta-versus-Google gap is a difference in on-site revenue, not a savings estimate.
