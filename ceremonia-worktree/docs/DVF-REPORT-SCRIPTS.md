# DVF report scripts

**Date:** 11 October 2026
**Site of the run:** DVF, idsite `5795`, `https://dvf.com`.
**Report they serve:** `docs/analysis/dvf/DVF-CLIENT-REPORT.md`, charts in `docs/analysis/dvf/charts/`.
**Copies:** `py/export.mjs`, `py/build.py`, `py/split.py`. The fourth copy, `py/charts.py`, was retired in Phase 3 of the orchestrator; `py/chart-render.py` draws report charts from any full bundle.

These scripts turn a warehouse extract and the rows already written by this project into the evidence bundle behind that report. They run after the pipeline. They are not adapters, and they do not write `ae_dataset_row_current` or `ae_trend_row`.

The copies keep the paths from the 11 October run. They are a reference for the method. Point them at a new site and a new window before running them again.

`py/hv.py` was not copied. The original is an eight-line filter that prints high-value pages whose URLs match one pattern. The same table is already in the bundle (`high_value_page_reach` in `05_pages_and_high_value.txt`).

## `py/export.mjs` — warehouse extracts

One client, six queries, each written to `/tmp/pc/<name>.json`. Pass job names as arguments to rerun a subset. Settings cap each query at 600 seconds.

| Job | Grain | Window | What it keeps |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `orders` | one row per order | 1 Aug – 10 Oct 2026 | `cityHash64` of the Shopify order id, visit id, revenue, device, and one item title/price/qty from `conversion_item`. DVF's item table has one item row per order, so multi-item baskets are not itemised. |
| `history_extra` | one row per visit with a device id | 12–25 Aug | Pageviews, time, max scroll, clicks, add-to-cart, checkout, orders. This is the lookback `build.py` needs for a 30-day history on visits from 14 Sep. |
| `visit_scroll_max` | one row per visit | 26 Aug – 9 Oct | Max `scroll_y_max_relative` for the current window. |
| `page_period` | URL × period × device, 10+ visits | 1 Sep – 9 Oct | Visits, buyer visits, entries, entry-buyer visits. Buyer means the visit ordered, not that the page caused the order. |
| `hv_page_reach` | URL, 100+ visitors or 3+ high-value visitors | 10 Sep – 9 Oct | Share of device ids with an order of $1,000 or more who saw the page, against the share of all visitors. |
| `pipeline_datasets` | one stored pipeline row | whatever the batch holds for idsite 5795 | `dataset_id`, dimensions, `dim_extra`, `metrics` from `journey_events.ae_dataset_row_current`. Prefers `exclude_confirmed` over `none` when both exist for a dataset. |

Page class is assigned in SQL from the first page-rule URL on `site_hsr`: `home`, `private_sale`, `sale_coll`, `product`, `new_fall_coll`, `other_coll`, `search`, `cart`, `size_guide`, `account`, `content`, `other`. The visit filter used here drops desktop visits whose referrer is `facebook` with an empty medium and an empty campaign. That is the automated burst called out in the report.

From `dvf_ck.pkl`: `idvisit` and every column whose name starts with `c_`.

`d` is a datetime. `bot` is boolean. `buy` is 1 when the visit contains an order.

## `py/build.py` — the analysis

```
python3 py/build.py /path/to/bundle.json
```

Reads the pickles and the JSON extracts. Writes one JSON object. Each table inside it is `{description, grain, source, row_count, columns, rows}`, and `rows` are arrays aligned to `columns`.

The logic that is worth reusing sits in six places.

**Campaign-link channel.** `link_channel` classifies the link the visit arrived on, separately from the referrer channel already stored as `ch`. Priority is: automated email flow, affiliate, Google Ads (`cpc`), campaign email or SMS, Meta with a medium or campaign, Meta with neither, no campaign link, anything else tagged. Source is `utm` when set, otherwise `referer_name`.

**Periods.** `pre` is before 10 Sep, `full` is 10–23 Sep, `promo` is 24 Sep – 4 Oct, `post` is after that. `peak` (25–27 Sep) is a subset of `promo`, used only in the cohort table. Weeks are Mondays. The week of 25 Aug starts on 26 Aug, and the week of 5 Oct ends on 9 Oct.

**Price and discount.** For each product title, the full price is the median item price on orders from 10–22 Sep. An order is `marked_down` when its item price is below 95% of that median. `paid_to_item` is order revenue divided by item price times quantity. At full price that ratio sits near 1.07, because of tax and shipping. Below 1 means the shopper paid less than the item price.

**30-day shopper history.** Visits from 14 Sep onward are classed by what the same `config_id` did in the prior 30 days, using `history_extra` for 12–25 Aug and the current visits after that. Priority: `bought_before`, `left_bag` (add to cart or checkout), `browsed_deeply_last_visit_no_bag` (last prior visit had more than 3 pageviews, or a click plus 25% scroll plus more than 60 seconds), `browsed_before`. With nothing in the 30 days: `returning_last_seen_over_30d` or `first_visit`. Visits with an empty device id are `no_device_id` and should be left out of a history cut. The bundle's own reconciliation says this class is wider than the report's: any later visit on a device that has already bought counts as `bought_before`.

**Cohorts.** One long table, `visit_cohorts`, repeats the same measures (visits, buyer visits, orders, revenue, add-to-cart visits, checkout visits) for each behaviour value in each period. Families include channel, device, entry class, both history windows, scroll bands, journey prefix, country, and the `c_*` click flags. Click-flag rows cover `full` and `promo` only. Journeys are kept when the collapsed path has 200 or more visits.

**Pipeline rows.** `dim_extra` and `metrics` are parsed from JSON and prefixed `x_` and `m_`. CSS selectors are interned to `selector_id`. Four datasets are reduced before they are stored, because the raw rows are too wide for a portable file:

| Dataset | Reduction |
| --- | --- |
| `trigger-chains` | 61,038 per-visit rows become hit date × trigger × device × level × final. `final` is 1 when the 14-day window had closed by 10 Oct. |
| `dwell-model-inputs` | Per-session homepage dwell becomes week × device, with median and 75th percentile engaged time. |
| `rage-clicks` | Totals by week × device × element class, plus the selectors that had at least one rage session. |
| `tap-geometry-weekly` | Selector-weeks with 3 or more clicks. |
| `render-snapshot` | Nodes with on-screen width and height. |

`audit-template`, `audit-funnel`, and `descriptor-snapshot` are dropped. The report does not use them.

The rest of the file is the glossary (`definitions`), the map from report section to table (`report_section_index`), and `reconciliation`. That last block is part of the method. It records which headline numbers match exactly, which were recomputed under different counting, and which lines in the report disagree with the bundle. On this run the disagreements include the "87% of $1,000+ orders were a first visit" line (the visit-count field is 1 on every order), the Meta desktop order count (5 in one place, 2 in another), and the share of bag returns attributed to an automated flow.

## `py/split.py` — the handoff files

```
python3 py/split.py /path/to/bundle.json /path/to/outdir
```

Splits the bundle into the files an analyst or a model can open without a database. `01_orders.csv` is plain CSV. The other files wrap each table in markers:

```
=== TABLE: daily_site ===
description: …
grain: …
source: …
--- BEGIN CSV ---
…rows…
@@ daily_site | columns: …     # every 100 rows, a repeated header
--- END CSV ---
```

Skip lines that start with `@@` when parsing. The loader for that format is written into `00_README_data_guide.md`. The script asserts that every table in the bundle is placed in some file, so a new table in `build.py` has to be added to `FILES` or the split fails.

| `03_weekly_channels.txt` | `weekly_entry_cube`, `weekly_source_campaign`, `meta_creative_period` |

## Using this on a later analysis

The method is: extract the orders, the lookback, and the pipeline rows; classify visits once; aggregate to the grains the narrative needs; write the definitions and the mismatches into the same bundle; split that bundle into files a reader can open; chart from those files.

For another site or another month, change these and leave the shape alone.

1. **Site and window** in `export.mjs`: `idsite`, the `dvf.com` URL stem, and every `BETWEEN` date. The page-class `multiIf` is DVF's URL scheme. Replace it with the classes that site actually uses.
2. **Visit export.** Rebuild `dvf.pkl` and `dvf_ck.pkl` (or change the paths) so the columns listed above exist for the new window. This is the step the saved scripts skip.
3. **Periods and the full-price baseline** in `build.py`. The 10–22 Sep median is the baseline only because that stretch was full price on DVF. Another promotion needs its own baseline dates, or markdown detection will treat the sale price as the full price.
4. **Channel rules** only where the site's UTM scheme differs. Keep two channels: how the tracker saw the referrer, and how the campaign link was tagged. The report's useful finding was the gap between them.
5. **History.** Keep the priority order (bought, left a bag, browsed deeply, browsed, none). State in `reconciliation` whether a post-purchase visit counts as `bought_before`. That choice moved the class sizes on this run.
6. **Pipeline reductions.** Keep the aggregations for `trigger-chains` and `dwell-model-inputs`. Add a new dataset to `FILES` in `split.py` or the assert will fail.
7. **Charts.** Point `D` and `OUT` at the new split directory and the new charts folder. Rebuild figure 3 from `daily_channel_device` (sum revenue / sum visits by `link_ch` over the window) instead of pasting the bars. Keep the 45% discount-day rule only if you have checked, on this site, that full-price days really do sit near 30–35% paid-below-item.
8. **Reconciliation.** Fill `exact_matches` and `recomputed_with_different_counting` from the new run. A bundle that cannot say where it disagrees with the prose is not ready to hand off.

Dependencies for the Python steps are pandas, numpy, and, for the charts, matplotlib. Homebrew's Python refuses a plain `pip install`. The install that works on this machine is `python3 -m pip install --user --break-system-packages matplotlib pandas numpy`.

## What belongs in the engine

A comparison that will be asked again on every site belongs in `ae_trend_row`, as described in `docs/VISION.md`: traffic source, new versus returning, buyer versus not, week over week, on a journey, a page, or an element. `build.py` had to recompute those cuts because the stored datasets do not put path, week, and source on one row.

These stay report scripts:

- A promotion calendar and a markdown test against a chosen full-price baseline.
- Paid-versus-item-price as a way to see a checkout discount when the discount code is not in the tracking data.
- A high-value buyer defined as an order over a dollar line, and the pages those buyers read.
- The portable-file format and the reconciliation block.

`docs/ANALYSIS-ROADMAP.md` and `docs/DVF-OPPORTUNITIES.md` are the pass over the strategy rows and the homepage datasets. This file is the pass that joined those datasets to orders and to shopper history for the client report.
