# Report style guide

How the Site Opportunity Report, Today Opportunities, and User Journey Opportunity Analysis should read. The specimens are the Word reports in `~/work/analysis/reports/` (Loop Earplugs, Latico Leathers, DVF). Loop is the format reference. Section order and what to compute live in the three document skills and in `skills/analysis-doc-standards.md`. This file is the voice, the page, and the way a number is allowed to appear.

Write for the operator who can change the site or the ad account this week. They already know their products. They need the leak, the size, and the move.

## The three documents

They are one analysis in three lengths. Each has a job, and each leaves the others' job alone.

| Document | Job | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Site Opportunity Report | The month: what happened, the opportunities, the 30-day plan. | The long document. |
| Today Opportunities | Three settings that can be changed this morning. | About an hour of work. |
| User Journey Opportunity Analysis | How shoppers move, and where that path leaks. | The path, not a second action list. |

Today opens by saying the full analysis found the leaks and this document is today's fix. The journey report names an opportunity from the Site Opportunity Report when the leak is already specified there, and does not repeat its action list. Today puts the larger plays in section 06 and labels them as the week's queue.

If the site has fewer than three live settings leaks, write the ones that qualify and say the rest are queued. If the bundle supports fewer than eight opportunities, stop. Do not pad a section to fill a template.

## Voice

A CRO analyst, speaking in the first person plural for the work ("We followed every cart abandoned between 12 and 27 September") and in the imperative for the action ("Pause the losers"). The reader is not addressed as "you should".

Lead with the finding, then the number that makes it true, then the move. A paragraph that opens with background and arrives at the point at the end is the wrong shape.

Name the thing. Campaign names, URLs, page titles, and element names appear as they do in the data: `EU_EN Archive Sale`, `/pages/archive-access-paid`, the gallery arrows. A finding about "paid social" that never names the campaign is unfinished.

Use the merchant's noun for the cart. Loop says cart. Latico and DVF say bag. Say checkout when the data is checkout.

House spelling is British: behaviour, colour. Currency is the order file's, with a symbol and thousands separators.

Short sentences. One idea each. An em dash carries a short aside, not a second essay. Middle dots pack a split onto one line: `Mobile 82% · Tablet 10% · Desktop 8%`.

State the place the break sits. "The gap sits between cart and checkout" is the sentence. Follow it with the two rates. A reader who might look at the top of the funnel gets the matching rate in the same breath: mobile adds to cart at 13.9%, desktop at 13.8%.

Interpret in the merchant's terms. "These are ready buyers fidgeting at the moment of commitment." "The repeat buyer is not a segment of this business; it is the business." One such sentence per finding is enough. Do not stack metaphors.

## Numbers

Every number is anchored. The anchor is the prior fortnight, a peer in the same table (device, channel, page, journey), or the next funnel step. A rate printed alone is unfinished.

Prose rounds; tables keep the exact figure. "About 180,000 shoppers a month" in the sentence, `97,297` in the table. Use "about", "roughly", and "on the order of" for a monthly size. Use the exact rate when the gap is the finding: `17.1%` against `22.0%`, `$0.84` against `$2.55`.

One decimal when the gap is wide. Two when it is tight (`4.64%` against `6.08%`). A range uses an en dash: `$4.72–$5.99`, `6.5–8.1%`, `$0.19–$0.30`. A multiple is words when it is the point: "35 to one", "ten to sixteen times", "a tenth of the European rate". A share is a fraction in prose when it is the point: "one visit in five", "four in five", "half of all orders".

Weekly tables start on Monday. A partial week is labelled in the row: `7 Sep (from 12th)`, `5 Oct (through 11th)`. "Last week" in Today Opportunities is that Monday–Sunday week, and the method section says which days it actually contains.

Dollar sizes are monthly estimates from this window's volumes. Call them upper bounds. Say what behaviour the estimate assumes, in the sentence or in the method: re-pointed traffic converts like the destination's existing traffic; shoppers who browse further are partly different people. "Up to about $50,000 a month" is the shape. "This will generate $50,000" is not.

Scale the dollars to this site. A small store states the counts beside the rate, and says when one order moves the rate. A segment under about 100 visits or about 10 orders is directional. On a large site, a segment that is thin relative to that site is directional.

Last touch is said when a channel looks weak or strong: the visit is credited to the link it arrived on. Revenue per visit is on-site value. Ad spend is not in the data, so a sentence about profit names that limit or frames the check as something to pull in Ads Manager.

## The page

### Header

Site Opportunity Report:

```
HEATMAP
{Brand}
Site Opportunity Report
{domain}  |  {D Month} – {D Month YYYY}, with {context} for context  |  Prepared {D Month YYYY}
```

One sentence on what was re-analysed: orders, human visits, and the datasets. Name the analysed page if the click and scroll record is for one page.

Today Opportunities and the journey report put the plan or the analysis title in the Heatmap line, then the brand and domain, then the prepared line in small capitals in the body:

```
PREPARED OCTOBER 12, 2026 · DATA THROUGH OCTOBER 11, 2026
```

Today's subtitle is fixed: `A one-hour ad-account intervention · three switches that change tomorrow's numbers`.

Date style follows the document. The Site Opportunity Report uses day month (`12 September – 11 October 2026`). Today and the journey report use month day (`October 12, 2026`, `September 12 – October 11, 2026`).

### Titles

An opportunity title is a claim the operator can act on: "Bring back the shoppers who leave a full bag", "Make every paid-social dollar accountable". It is not a topic label.

Today's title is the leak in plain language: `Today's Plan: Stop Paying for Visits That Can't Convert`.

Journey section titles name this site's story. "How the Slowdown and the Drops Reshaped the Funnel" and "How the Sales Reshaped the Funnel" are the same section. The default names are in the journey skill; rewrite them when the data has a better noun.

### Contents

The Site Opportunity Report lists the sections in one line, separated by middle dots, including each opportunity by its title.

Today and the journey report use a numbered contents list: `01` through `06` or `07`, then `A` for Data and Method. Section headings in the body repeat that number: `01  The Situation This Morning`.

### The biggest wins

Six numbered moves. The opening sentence says they cover most of the opportunity, then names the kind of move in a clause each ("one raises the value of every order"). Each item is an imperative headline, one paragraph of evidence, and `(Opportunity N)` at the end. No number appears here that is not in the opportunity below it.

Close with the supporting plays that did not make the six, each pointed at its opportunity.

### At a glance

One table, about ten rows: `#`, `Finding`, `Recommended action`, `Priority`. Priority is `High`, `Medium`, or `Watch`. The finding holds the comparison. The action is a verb phrase an owner can hand to someone.

### The month in numbers

A definition list, then weekly orders, then the daily-orders figure, then the paragraph that reads the chart, then "Two contexts matter for reading the month." Those two contexts are the comparisons that change how every later number should be read: volume against the prior period, and where demand concentrates.

### An opportunity

Three parts, in this order, with these headings:

- **What we saw.** The table first, then short bullets that read it. The cohort, the device, the page next door, and the window are in the table or the sentence above it.
- **Why it matters.** One monthly size, as an upper bound, and the structural reason the gap persists.
- **Recommended actions.** Imperatives. Each one could be handed to an owner. The last items name the number to track and its value today.

### Today, one action

The heading is `02  Action 1 — {imperative}`. The first line is `Owner: paid media. Time: about 20 minutes.` Then one sentence on why this one is first and what happens every day it waits.

The steps are a morning in Ads Manager: the exact campaigns or URLs, what to change, and what not to do (re-point rather than pause, when that is the point). End with `Expected result:` and the upper bound, including the assumption that makes it an upper bound.

### Callouts

A callout is a labelled sentence, then the paragraph. The label is bold or set off with a colon. Use these labels for these jobs:

| Label | Job |
|---|---|
| Why these three and not the bigger ideas. | Today, under the opening. The larger work is worth more and takes longer. Tomorrow's spend is committed tonight. |
| How to read a journey. | Journey, under the opening. Define a journey for this site, including what a one-page visit can still contain. |
| So what: | Journey, at the end of a section. The move that follows from the table, and the upper bound beside it. |
| Watch out: | A measurement break inside a section. The event changed; here is the rate that stayed stable. |

### Tables

A header row, then the rows, then a caption on its own line: `Table 1 — The three live leaks, with the numbers as of the most recent week of tracked data. Sizes are monthly estimates from current volumes, not forecasts.`

The caption says what the table is, the window, and the one caveat a reader needs before using a cell. Column heads are short. Put the share or the count in the cell when both matter: `80,295 (82%)`.

Compare inside one table. Entry sessions and human visits are different denominators; they do not share a sentence.

### Figures

One sentence of caption. Say what the axes are, the window, and what the colour means.

`Figure 1. Daily orders, 29 August – 11 October 2026. Gold days are the three peak-revenue days of the window; grey days are the pre-window context.`

The Site Opportunity Report uses `Figure N.` with a period. Today and the journey report use `Figure N —` with an em dash. Gold marks the thing to notice: peak days, the cluster under a threshold, the high-value journeys. Grey marks context. Skip a chart when the table behind it is empty, and say so where the chart would have been.

A dollar sign in a matplotlib label is escaped. Axis labels do not overlap. The font is DejaVu Sans.

### What's working, and worth watching

Worth watching is a named line that moved and is not yet an opportunity. Each subsection is a title and a short paragraph that says what would make it one.

What's working opens with "We checked these areas closely. Each is healthy and needs no change today." Each line is a bold name and one sentence of the evidence. Include a line only when it was actually checked.

### The 30 days, and the rest of the week

Site Opportunity Report: `Week`, `Action`, `Owner`. Weeks are `1`, `2`, `3–4`, `4`. Owners are roles: `Paid media`, `E-commerce`, `E-commerce / Dev`, `CRM`, `Merchandising`.

Today, section 06: `When`, `Action`, `Size`. The days are Tue–Fri. The opening says these are deliberately the week's work, queued once the three switches are on.

### How to read tomorrow

Four rows: `Watch`, `Now`, `Target`. The prose before the table says direction should be visible tomorrow, and size needs three or four days on a large site, about a week on a small one. One caution follows the table: a promotion, drop, restock, or send inside the reading window moves orders on its own, and should be noted beside the numbers.

### Method

Each document ends with the method. The Site Opportunity Report calls it `Method and caveats`. The other two call it `A  Data and Method`.

State the window, including partial weeks and the context period. State the dead-session or automated-traffic rule, the count, and the share. State that a visit with an add to cart, a checkout, or an order is never excluded. State last touch, that sizes are upper bounds, and that buyers are counted by device. Name any break: an event definition that changed, a tagging gap, a partial line-item extract, a match rate between orders and tracked visits. Do not claim a measurement the method says is missing.

The journey method also says how a journey was built: page classes, consecutive repeats counted once, and what landed in `other`.

## In markdown

The documents in this repository are markdown with the same order and the same sentences.

- The Heatmap line, the brand, and the document title are bold lines.
- A section is a heading. Today and the journey report keep the `01` number in the heading.
- A callout is a blockquote. The label is bold.
- A table is a markdown table. The caption is the line under it: `Table 1 — …`
- A chart is an image in `media/`, then the caption in italics: `*Figure 1 — …*` or `*Figure 1. …*`
- Recommended actions and morning steps are a list of imperatives.

Do not add a preamble, a summary box, or a "key takeaways" block beyond the biggest-wins list and the at-a-glance table. Those two already are the summary.
