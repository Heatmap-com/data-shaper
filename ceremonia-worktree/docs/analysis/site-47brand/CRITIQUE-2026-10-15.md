# Critique — '47 reports (Site Opportunity, Today Opportunities, User Journey)

**Reviewed:** 15 October 2026, against the analyst bundle at `~/work/analysis/site-47brand` (full folder).
**Method:** every headline number in the three reports was either (a) recomputed from the bundle files (`01_orders.csv`, `02_daily_and_campaigns.txt`, `03_weekly_channels.txt`, `05_pages_and_high_value.txt`, `06_shoppers_and_cohorts.txt`), or (b) marked *not independently verifiable* where the bundle lacks the underlying visit-level aggregates. Charts were checked against the tables they render.
**Verdict:** the core findings are **solid and overwhelmingly verify exactly** — the errors found are in secondary sizing math, cross-report consistency, and a few captions, not in the headline analyses. The reports' real weakness is that three documents were computed on slightly different pipelines without a shared, anchored "month in numbers."

## 2. Errors and inconsistencies (fix before these circulate further)

### 2.1 Computational error — the search opportunity is overstated ~39% (SOR Opportunity 6)

> "If half of the single-page search visits found one more page and converted at the site rate of 2.19%, that is up to about $180,000 a month."

The stated premise gives: single-page search visits = 253,203 × 73.7% = 186,614. Half = 93,307. At 2.19% × $63.32 = **$129,000/month**, not $180,000. The $180k only arises if you take half of *all* search visits (126,602 × 2.19% × $63.32 = $175.6k) — which contradicts the sentence's own premise. Either rewrite the premise ("half of all search visits") or restate the size as ~$130k. Note also the irony that 5.03% of search visits *already* buy — the counterfactual baseline deserves one clause.

### 2.2 Cross-report error — the flagship campaign's visit count (Today Action 1)

- SOR: campaign `21213618445` = **66,139 visits** at $0.33 → matches the bundle exactly (66,139; $22,161 including the one pre-window day of the week of 14 Sep; $0.34 computed).
- Today: "**74,386 visits** across the window at $0.34" → **overstated by 12.4%** vs the bundle. $0.34 × 74,386 = $25.3k of implied revenue vs $21.8–22.2k actual — the figure doesn't reconcile with the order file either. The SOR number is the verified one; Action 1's dollar bound (~$85k) should be recomputed on 66,139 if the visit base changes it.

### 2.3 UJ header mislabels the dead-session count's scope

UJ's header: "2,606,326 dead sessions (41.4% of recorded traffic) are excluded throughout." That is the **Sep 1–Oct 14 coverage** figure (verified: 2,606,326 of 6,300,888 = 41.4%). The report window — which every UJ table actually uses — is 1,868,538 of 4,397,274 = **42.5%** (SOR's number, verified). The UJ tables use window human visits (2,528,736) while citing the coverage dead count. One number is right for one scope and wrong for the claim; state the window figure, or state both with scopes.

### 2.4 The two reports contradict each other on what the peak days are

- SOR: "That is game-calendar demand, **not a code**," citing marked-down share 1.5% on peaks vs 3.5% window (this verifies — §1).
- UJ §04: "A lift across every channel at once is **a promotion or a code**, not a retention story, and this bundle carries no promotion calendar to confirm it."

Both cite the same NFL dates. SOR's markdown evidence is the better argument and is verified; UJ's sentence should be aligned (e.g., "broad lift is consistent with a code, but the markdown share on the peak days ran below the window, so game-calendar demand is the better read"). As written, a client reading both reports sees the analysts disagreeing on a load-bearing interpretation.

### 2.5 Caption error — Today Figure 1's weekly range

Caption: "Meta paid has run every week at **$0.52–$0.76** while the site held $1.12–$1.58." The chart itself plots $0.65 / $0.74 / $0.77 / $0.66 (verified from `03`) — observed range **$0.65–$0.77**. Both bounds are wrong; the upper bound understates the latest full week ($0.77, week of 5 Oct), which slightly weakens the "still leaking this week" narrative's precision even though the conclusion holds.

### 2.6 Welcome-flow revenue definition drifts

Bundle (`02` campaign_daily, all `Welcome_1` variants): SMS $68,967, email $37,235. SOR: $59,464 / $33,595 (−14% / −10%). Conversion rates and RPV agree within rounding, so the difference is **which campaign-name variants count as "the welcome flow"** — the report doesn't define the match. One line in Method ("welcome flow = campaigns matching `Welcome_1*`" or the exact list) fixes it.

## 3. Internal tensions that need one reconciliation pass

1. **Two checkout-completion rates, unreconciled.** SOR Opportunity 2 sizes the $740k bound on mobile **checkout-to-order 48.3%**; UJ §04 says **orders per checkout fell from ~0.45 to ~0.37** by mid-October. Both are "verified-ish" cuts (0.45 is window-average orders/checkout across devices; 0.37 is the trend's endpoint; 48.3% is mobile-only), but no reader can reconcile 0.37 with 0.48, and the $740k bound silently assumes the *higher* number persists. State which rate sizes the bound, and re-size with 0.37 to show sensitivity (at 0.37 the bound drops ~23%).
2. **SOR Table 7 omits the `other` channel** (~2,491 matched orders, ~$164k, ≈$2.16 RPV — computable from `01_orders.csv`). The listed rows sum to 52,868 of 55,725 matched orders. For a table whose purpose is "channel value per visit," a mid-table channel silently absent is material; UJ §01's mix shares (39+25+19+10 = 93%) leave the same 7% unexplained. Add the row or a footnote.
3. **Chains "a month" vs cohort.** Table 4's cohort is triggers from **16 days** (15–30 Sep). SOR calls $897,461 "close to $0.9 million a month" — steady-state monthlyizing gives ~$1.7M, so the *label* is conservative, but "every purchase-rate point on the cart file is roughly 980 orders, about $62,000 a month" uses cohort counts (979 = 1% of 97,873) as if monthly (a month of cart chains ≈ 183k → 1pt ≈ $116k). Opportunity 2/3 size on 30-day volume; Opportunity 1 sizes on 16-day cohort volume. Pick one annualization rule and state it.
4. **UJ window RPV $1.43 vs `daily_site` $1.41.** Trivial (matched-revenue base differences), but the two reports quote the same quantity two ways.

## 4. Not independently verifiable from the bundle (provenance gaps)

These report claims rest on visit-level aggregates **not present in the bundle files**. They may be correct (they come from the same warehouse), but an auditor cannot confirm them, and the reports don't say how they were computed:

- Device funnel rates: mobile ATC 9.2% / cart→checkout 49.8% vs desktop 12.3% / 75.6% (Table 5) — the bundle has device *visits* but no device-level ATC/checkout split.
- Single-page shares: 73.7% (search), 89% (Fall Collection social entries) — Fall Collection's 89.09% *does* verify from `09` (189,360/212,557); search's 73.7% does not appear anywhere derivable.
- Journey-pattern tables (UJ Table 2), scroll depths (21.4% reach 20%, 3.5% reach foot), "collection → product → home at 24.2% ($14.99)" — **no N given for this cell**; the 24.2% likely sits on a small base.
- Meta creative rows in Table 8 (sum to 399,296 of 968,607 Meta paid visits — **59% of Meta paid visits are not in the "Meta creatives" table**; worth a "remaining creatives" row so the table reads as a partition, not a sample).
- "Five postseason creatives: 42 orders from 57,305 visits" — a substring match on team names in `utm_content` finds 51 orders, so the exact creative-name set isn't reproducible from the bundle; define it.
- High-value device reach "38–48×", quick-buy $23,665 on 113,850 sessions, email 3+ click rates, popup flag absence.
- Google Ads "$2.16/visit at 3.2% conversion" (What's working) — bundle computes $2.22–$2.44 RPV and ~2.5% conversion for google_ads depending on the cut; the report's cut is undefined.

**Recommendation:** add a provenance column (or Method subsection) marking each table as *bundle-derived* (with the file/table) vs *query-derived* (with the SQL label), the same way the F&E analysis documented its runs. This is the single biggest auditability improvement available.

## 5. Analytical critiques (the findings hold; the framing can be tightened)

1. **Causal over-reach in two places.** O4: "Untagged organic social, the same audiences arriving without a paid link, converts at 3.63% — **the audiences are fine; the buys are not.**" O5: "Collections that convert **prove the traffic is fine**." Organic-social visitors are self-selected fans and navigators; they are not "the same audiences" as paid clickers, and converting collections don't prove anything about the *paid* cohort's intent. The conclusion (re-point spend) stands on the creative-level spread alone ($0.04–$1.44 within paid). Reword to what the data shows: broad creative outperforms named-team creative 25:1 *within the same paid channel* — that comparison is apples-to-apples; the organic comparison is not.
2. **The $740k bound leans on the shakiest verified number in the report.** It assumes recovered mobile carts convert at 48.3% checkout-to-order — the very event family UJ flags as drifting (0.45→0.37). Also "ready buyers fidgeting at the moment of commitment" is a story, not evidence; the drawer-diagnosis recommendations are standard best practice, but nothing in the data (no rage clicks, no field data, no session replay cited) identifies *which* fix addresses the 49.8%. Present the bound with the drift caveat and rank the fixes as hypotheses to A/B, not as the read of the data. (Rage-click data exists in the bundle's pipeline and shows <1/1000 — the report does cite this; good.)
3. **Opportunity 8's unmatched-orders attribution** ("roughly $87,000 belongs to Meta paid") is presented as neutral but unmatched orders plausibly skew to checkout-typed/direct sessions (consent-blocked post-purchase tracking) — i.e., *away* from paid last-touch. One sentence of caveat.
4. **Opportunity 3 lacks any basket evidence.** The orders file has item titles, quantities, and a multi-item flag — yet no analysis of what the 26,410 sub-$110 orders *contain* or what the marginal second item costs. "Cap-plus-tee at $75–$95" is asserted, not derived. A 10-line basket-composition table (top second-item pairs on sub-$110 orders) would turn this from a hunch into a finding.
5. **No uncertainty discipline on small cells**, despite the platform's own floor rules. Cells like Chicago market (84 visits, $0), `/pages/the-everyday-franchise` (6,940 entries, 0.04% ≈ 3 buyers), Threads-style placements, and the 24.2% journey are quoted with the same confidence as 250k-visit channels. Add Ns to every journey table and a floor note (the engine's own rule: ≥30 sessions for a rate) in Method.
6. **Dead-session definition is bespoke and disclosed, but under-analyzed.** "42.5% dead" is enormous; the reports treat dead sessions as a data-quality footnote (O8) rather than asking *which channels they inflate*. If dead share differs by channel (paid social is the usual suspect), every channel RPV ranking shifts after correction. One table — dead share by channel — belongs in O8. This is the largest unanswered analytical question in the reports.
7. **No reconciliation of the analyst "dead session" rule against the engine's shipped bot tiers** (exclude_confirmed). The reports note the two filters differ (Method, O8) but stop there; O8's own action item calls for exactly this reconciliation, so the report should either show the overlap table or flag it as blocked.

## 6. Process recommendations (for the report-writing context)

1. **Fix the three hard errors**: §2.1 (search $180k → $129k or reword), §2.2 (Today 74,386 → 66,139 and re-check the $85k bound), §2.3 (UJ dead-session scope). Then §2.4 (align the peak-day interpretation) and §2.5 (caption range).
2. **Generate one shared "month in numbers" block** (orders, revenue, human visits, dead count+share, device mix, channel table incl. `other`, matched/unmatched) from a single query, and have all three reports quote it. Most of §2–§4 dies automatically when the three reports can't drift.
3. **Adopt the anchor pattern** the F&E analysis used (`00_site_profile.json` anchors with expected values): a machine-checkable anchor file killed three would-be errors in this review before they reached paper. Verify anchors on every bundle rebuild.
4. **One sizing convention.** Every dollar figure either scales to 30 days from stated volumes or is labeled "cohort." Apply uniformly (fixes §3.3).
5. **Add Ns and floor labels** to all journey/creative/market cells.
6. **Provenance marking** per table (bundle file + table, or query label) per §4.
7. **Charts:** figures matched their tables (verified Figure 1 axis values); add a render-time check that captions are generated from the same data arrays as the plots — the Figure 1 caption error (§2.5) is exactly the kind of drift that automated caption generation prevents.
8. **Cross-report QA pass:** a final diff-style read of the three Method sections for any pair of sentences asserting different numbers for the same quantity (this review found five: campaign visits, dead count, Google Ads RPV, peak-day interpretation, checkout-completion rate).

## 7. What *not* to change

The disclosure discipline is excellent and should be treated as the template: every dollar figure labeled "upper bound, not a forecast" with its assumption stated; partial weeks labeled everywhere; the "Worth watching" instrumentation caveat on desktop checkout-to-order (which later data may prove right); the bought-before-flag self-count catch; the "no confirmed promotion calendar" honesty; dead-session and last-touch definitions in Method. The trigger-chain cohort design (closed 14-day windows, 16-day trigger cohort) is textbook. The headline opportunities — Meta creative tiering, the mobile cart gap, Fall Collection as a paid dead end, the chains device gap, replenishment — all verify against the bundle, and the sizing bounds that check (chains, Fall Collection, replenishment, campaign pause) are arithmetically honest. The reports are good; they are one shared-compute layer and one consistency pass away from being audit-proof.
