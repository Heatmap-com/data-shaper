## Opportunity 1 — Bring back the shoppers who leave a full bag

**What we saw**

We followed every shopper who added to bag or started checkout between 11 and 26 September without buying. Each one was followed for 14 days.

| | Shoppers | Bought within 14 days | Came back |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Abandoned bag, all devices | 6,123 | **730 (11.9%)** | about two in three |

- The 730 recovered buyers spent **$176,617**, about $242 each — counted once per shopper.
- **Mobile holds 74% of these shoppers.** Recovery is a phone experience here before anything else.
- Recovery is **slow**: 206 of the 730 bought within an hour, 372 within a day, and the remaining third spread across the full two weeks — 189 of them after day 7. A single same-day email reaches the easy third and misses the rest.
- The automated email flows are already the highest-value traffic on the site (up to $17.88 per visit on back-in-stock messages) — they just reach too few people.

**Why it matters**

Bag abandoners are the largest warm audience the site has: roughly ten thousand shoppers a month. Every point of recovery on this group is about 61 more orders — call it $13,000 — and the current 11.9% is earned with little more than a single automated flow.

**Recommended actions**

1. **Build a sequenced recovery flow that covers the full two weeks:** a same-hour reminder, a day-one follow-up, and at least two more touches in week two. The data says the money is spread across all fourteen days.
2. **Design for mobile first:** SMS or push where you have consent, with a one-tap link back to a saved bag.
3. **Keep the bag between visits**, and greet a returning bag-holder with their items rather than a generic homepage.
4. **Grow the automated flows.** They earn more per visit than anything else on the site and are currently the smallest channel.
5. **Track it as one number:** the share of abandoned bags that become orders within 14 days. Today that is 11.9%.

## Opportunity 2 — The Callie page is the front door; build the basket there

**What we saw**

The most-visited page on the site is not the homepage. It is the **Callie Sling Crossbody** product page:

| Page | Peak-week visits | Typical order rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| /products/callie-sling-crossbody | **14,113** | 1.6–2.6% |
| Homepage | 12,517 | 3.5–3.9% |
| /collections/best-sellers | 7,216 | 2.7–3.3% |
| /collections/crossbody | 6,489 | 3.0–3.8% |
| /collections/new-arrivals | 6,448 | 2.3–3.2% |

- In the sampled order lines, **42 of 57 items sold were the Callie.** One bag carries the door.
- **Search is the page's gold channel**: search-referred visits to the Callie page earn **$9.80–$13.32 each**, with ~60 orders a week — two to three times the page's next-best channel.
- **Meta is buying more of the page and earning less**: Meta-referred Callie visits rose from 1,588 to 3,770 across the window while revenue per visit slid from $3.43 to $2.62.
- In the week of 21 September the page absorbed a large wave of non-buying visits (see Worth watching) and its measured order rate halved while orders held — the page itself was fine.

**Why it matters**

A one-product front door is a strength until something disturbs it — a stock-out, a ranking change, a review. The protective move is to raise the value of every Callie visit while the traffic is there.

**Recommended actions**

1. **Build attach on the Callie page.** The site already sells "Free Returns Package Protection" as an add-on (327 sold in the window), so the mechanism works. Add "complete the look" pairings and a second-item incentive at a threshold. With a $215 average order, every point of attach rate is real money (see Opportunity 4).
2. **Protect the search placements that feed the page**, and extend the same play to the next heroes (Darby, Crosby, Petite Lindsey) — they already show the same gallery-driven behavior as Callie.
3. **Audit the Meta campaign that lands on the Callie page.** Visits are up, value is down; either the audiences changed or the creative promise does not match the page.
4. **Watch the hero's back.** If Callie stock or rankings ever slip, the site's front door goes with it. A second hero product in waiting is cheap insurance.

## Opportunity 3 — Fix the product gallery: the frustration has one address

**What we saw**

Repeated rapid clicking — the classic sign of a control that isn't doing what the shopper expects — is rare on this site (roughly 120–160 sessions a week, steady all month). But it is not spread around:

- **92% of all frustration clicks land on a single control**: the image-gallery arrows/dots on product pages.
- **Desktop and tablet shoppers produce 639 of those sessions; mobile produces 1.** Touch users swipe through the gallery; pointer users fight it.
- The shoppers who rage-click the gallery are the site's best: **6% of them buy anyway** — four to five times the normal rate — in spite of the control. These are ready buyers fidgeting at the moment of commitment.
- Every major product page shows the same pattern, so this is one shared theme component, not a page problem.

**Recommended actions**

1. **Open any product page on a desktop browser and work the gallery arrows.** The frustration is concentrated enough that the failure should reproduce quickly — dead clicks, unresponsive dots, or a hover/click mismatch.
2. **Fix it at the theme level** so every product page inherits the repair.
3. **Count frustrated sessions weekly.** The fix works when the ~120–160 weekly figure collapses; the gallery's share of it should go first.

## Opportunity 4 — Basket size is the growth lever

**What we saw**

The order distribution has no luxury tail: 54 orders all month were $500 or more, and the average sits at $215 (median $208). Growth from new customers is expensive; growth from bigger baskets is arithmetic: **at 3,715 orders a month, every +$10 of average order is about $37,000 a month.**

**Recommended actions**

1. **A basket threshold just above today's average** — test $250 with a clear incentive (free shipping threshold, a gift, or a modest bundle discount).
2. **Bundles on the collection pages** that already convert at 3%+ (best-sellers, crossbody): two-piece pairings at a small saving.
3. **Attach on the hero page** (Opportunity 2) — the highest-traffic place to move the number.

## Opportunity 5 — Channel shifts worth acting on

Trend across the window, homepage and key pages:

| Channel | Direction | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Search | **Strengthening** | Homepage search visits earned $6.27 per visit in the stub week, then $7.51, $9.52, $8.91, $8.84. The Callie page earns $10–13 per search visit. |
| Meta | Mixed | Tiny but efficient on the homepage ($23–28 per visit in two weeks on small volume); on the Callie page, more visits and falling value ($3.43 → $2.62). |
| Social | **Softening on the homepage** | $13.75 per visit in the week of 14 Sep, then $8.08, $5.81, $7.98, while visits rose. |
| Direct | Healthy | $8–8.70 most weeks; dipped to $6.64 in the wave week. |
| Pinterest / Axon | Weak | $1.61 and $0.51 per visit respectively — trimming candidates. |
| Email flows | Strongest per-visit | Klaviyo $15.58, back-in-stock $17.88 — the smallest channels earning the most. |

**Recommended actions:** put review time into search placements (rising, highest intent); audit the Meta-to-Callie campaign (Opportunity 2); refresh social creative or audiences; shift marginal Pinterest/Axon budget toward email-flow growth; treat the automated flows as an acquisition channel worth expanding.

## Worth watching

**8 September: four times a normal day.** 536 orders and $111,295 in revenue on a single day, two days before this window opens, with no comparable day on either side. Whatever ran that day — an email, a sale, a feature, a creator — earned more in 24 hours than most optimizations deliver in a quarter. Name it, and put it on the calendar.

**The week-of-21-September desktop wave.** Desktop visits ran several times their normal level that week (visible even on the homepage, where direct visits peaked at 4,330 against ~3,700) while orders held flat across the site. The Callie page's measured order rate halved that week purely as arithmetic. Worth identifying the source — a feed, a feature, or an untagged campaign — before reading anything into desktop trends from that week.

**Frustration trend.** Steady at ~120–160 frustrated sessions a week all month. If Opportunity 3's fix lands, this line should break downward — it is the cheapest weekly quality metric on the site.

## What's working

We checked these areas closely. Each is healthy and needs no change today.

- **First-time buyers.** New visitors order at 2.84% against 3.26% for returning visitors — nearly equal, and $6.45 vs $7.35 per visit. The first-visit experience converts; there is no loyalty gap to fix.
- **Volume stability.** 845–904 orders in every full week, average order flat at $208–$221. No promotion was needed to hold the line.
- **Search momentum.** Rising earnings per visit four weeks running, on the homepage and the hero page alike.
- **Recovery headroom.** An 11.9% fourteen-day bag recovery is a strong base to build the sequenced flow on.
- **Frustration is contained.** One control owns nearly all of it — the rare frustration problem with a fixable address.
